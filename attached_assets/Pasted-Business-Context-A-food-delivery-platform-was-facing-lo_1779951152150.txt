Business Context

A food delivery platform was facing low order conversion due to decision fatigue caused by too many restaurant and menu choices. To improve customer decision-making, the product team introduced social-proof badges such as “Popular” and “Most Ordered” and ran a 14-day A/B experiment across 88K+ users.

Objective

The goal was to measure whether social-proof badges could increase order conversion and customer spending without negatively affecting cancellation rates or overall order quality.

Analysis & Metrics
Validated balanced traffic allocation between control and treatment groups before analysis
Evaluated key product metrics:
Conversion Rate → percentage of users placing orders
Average Order Value (AOV) → average spend per order
Cancellation Rate → percentage of cancelled orders after placement
Time-to-Order → average time users took to complete an order
Menu Exploration → number of menu interactions before ordering
Compared treatment performance across:
New vs returning users
Daily experiment trends over the 14-day period
Built an executive Power BI dashboard to communicate experiment performance and business impact clearly to stakeholders
Key Findings
Conversion rate improved from 20.17% to 20.86%, showing that social-proof badges helped more users complete purchases
Average order value increased from ₹322 to ₹346, indicating users spent more when exposed to popularity signals
Users completed purchases faster, reducing average decision time from 13 minutes to 12 minutes
Cancellation rates increased slightly but remained within acceptable levels, suggesting the additional orders were still high quality
New users responded more positively than returning users, showing social proof was especially effective for reducing uncertainty among first-time buyers
The treatment group explored more menus while still converting faster, suggesting improved confidence rather than rushed decision-making
Business Impact

The experiment generated an estimated ₹432K incremental revenue within 14 days, with projected monthly impact crossing ₹925K after full rollout.

A major insight from the analysis was the identification of novelty decay — the conversion uplift reduced significantly from Week 1 to Week 2, indicating the badge’s effectiveness weakened over time as users became familiar with it.

Recommendation

Recommended a phased rollout of social-proof badges with continued monitoring of long-term conversion trends. Also suggested testing alternative badge variations and prioritising exposure for new users, where the impact was strongest.
